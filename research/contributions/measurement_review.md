# Measuring Academic Overload and Student Burnout

**Working draft for team review — 22 October 2026.** Bibliographic details and the sections listed below were checked against open full texts on **21 October 2026 (Astana time)**. This is a thematic review, not an exhaustive systematic review. It contains no AITU participant data, local instrument validation, or project experiment results. This English edition preserves those source checks; no new web verification was performed during its preparation.

## Search and selection

The search was conducted on 21 October 2026 using web search followed by inspection of publisher pages. The queries actually used were:

| Query | Selection decision |
| --- | --- |
| `university student burnout measurement validation scale 2022 2023 2024 longitudinal study open access` | Identified BAT-S, UDRQ, and other instruments. M-01 and M-02 were included because they concerned university students and provided accessible full texts. |
| `academic overload burnout university students longitudinal 2021 2022 2023 study demands resources` | Identified studies of demands and resources. M-03 and M-05 were included, and the temporal design of M-03 was examined. |
| `"Time Pressure and Health-Related Loss" Frontiers 2021` | Located the publisher version of M-04 after a CAPTCHA prevented access through PMC; the available Frontiers HTML was read. |

Inclusion required a peer-reviewed journal publication, relevance to the measurement or temporal structure of student burnout or its associated factors, verifiable bibliographic information, and an accessible full text. Search results concerning social burnout, stress without a burnout outcome, or preprints alone were not included in these five records. The search was targeted and does not establish comprehensive coverage.

Four publications date from 2022–2024. M-04, published in April 2021, was retained because its panel design addresses a central methodological question, despite falling outside the strict five-year window of 21 October 2021–21 October 2026.

Locators below refer to published section and table titles, not search-tool line numbers. PDFs were not saved for this part of the review; local PDF paths and SHA-256 hashes are therefore not applicable. Open access to an article does not itself establish permission to deploy a third-party questionnaire. The shared [search log](../search_log.md), [bibliography](../bibliography.md), and [review protocol](../RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md) provide the wider context.

## M-01 — BAT-S: instrument properties and transfer limits

**Reference:** Carmona-Halty, M., Alarcón-Castillo, K., Semir-González, C., Sepúlveda-Páez, G., & Schaufeli, W. B. (2024). Burnout Assessment Tool for Students (BAT-S): evidence of validity in a Chilean sample of undergraduate university students. *Frontiers in Psychology, 15*, 1434412. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1434412) · [Full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1434412/full).

- **Publication and reading:** Peer-reviewed Brief Research Report; open HTML. Methods, Results, Discussion, and Tables 1–3 were read.
- **Question, sample, and outcome:** Psychometric evaluation of BAT-S in 461 Chilean undergraduate students. The 12 items cover four dimensions. The outcome is a current self-report, not a future ML label.
- **Method and split:** Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), gender measurement invariance, and structural equation modelling (SEM). Cross-sectional design; no separate predictive test set.
- **Reported results:** The respecified M4 model had CFI = 0.975 and RMSEA = 0.073. Overall omega was 0.874, whereas mental-distance omega was 0.689. These describe measurement properties, not forecasting accuracy.
- **Limitations:** Non-representative sampling, self-reports, and post hoc correlated errors. The study does not establish validity in Kazakhstan.
- **Project implication:** BAT-S is a candidate requiring checks of language, permissions, and local measurement properties. The paper does not establish diagnostic cut-offs for this project.
- **Locators:** Methods → Sample/Instruments; Results → Internal structure, Table 2 (M4), Reliability of the scores; Discussion → limitations paragraph.

## M-02 — Demands and resources as distinct factor groups

**Reference:** Jagodics, B., & Szabó, É. (2023). Student Burnout in Higher Education: A Demand-Resource Model Approach. *Trends in Psychology, 31*, 757–776. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43076-021-00137-4) · [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43076-021-00137-4). First published online on 6 January 2022; the volume publication year is 2023.

- **Publication and reading:** Peer-reviewed Original Article; open HTML. Methods, Results, Discussion, and Limitations and further research were read.
- **Question, sample, and outcome:** Development of the University Demand-Resource Questionnaire (UDRQ) and its associations with the Maslach Burnout Inventory–Student Survey (MBI-SS); a convenience sample of 743 Hungarian university students. MBI-SS measures concurrent state.
- **Method and split:** CFA, correlations, and SEM; cross-sectional data with no independent predictive test set.
- **Reported results:** The SEM explained R² = 0.664 for exhaustion, 0.721 for cynicism, and 0.636 for reduced accomplishment in the analysed sample. These are not estimates on future students.
- **Limitations:** Non-representative sampling, significance-based variable selection, and a need for longitudinal evidence.
- **Project implication:** Separate demands and resources blocks inform the candidate feature register. SEM “prediction” is not early individual forecasting; a demands-minus-resources difference is not adopted as a ready-made risk score.
- **Locators:** Methods → Instruments; Results → Main analysis: Model estimation, Fig. 1; Discussion → Limitations and further research.

## M-03 — Repeated cross-sections do not establish an individual panel

**Reference:** Salmela-Aro, K., Upadyaya, K., Ronkainen, I., & Hietajärvi, L. (2022). Study Burnout and Engagement During COVID-19 Among University Students: The Role of Demands, Resources, and Psychological Needs. *Journal of Happiness Studies, 23*, 2685–2702. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-022-00518-1) · [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-022-00518-1).

- **Publication and reading:** Peer-reviewed Research Paper; open HTML. Sections 6–12 were read, covering participants, measures, analysis, results, discussion, and limitations.
- **Question, samples, and outcome:** Comparison of University of Helsinki students in May 2020, December 2020, and April 2021; n = 1,501, 1,526, and 1,685. The ten-item Study Burnout Inventory had alpha = 0.94.
- **Method and split:** Linear mixed models, faculty-by-starting-year random intercepts, marginal R², and bootstrap confidence intervals. The three convenience samples have no established individual linkage across waves; no train/test split was used.
- **Reported result:** Burnout increased across the sampled periods, while the explanatory role of factors changed.
- **Limitations:** Pandemic context, one university, and self-selection. The authors explicitly describe each sample as cross-sectional.
- **Project implication:** Calendar and context warrant attention; this design does not demonstrate forecasting for an individual student.
- **Locators:** Section 6.1.1; Sections 7 and 8; Section 9/Table 2; Section 12, first paragraph.

## M-04 — Panel evidence on exhaustion over time

**Reference:** Gusy, B., Lesener, T., & Wolter, C. (2021). Time Pressure and Health-Related Loss of Productivity in University Students: The Mediating Role of Exhaustion. *Frontiers in Public Health, 9*, 653440. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2021.653440) · [Full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.653440/full).

- **Publication and reading:** Peer-reviewed Original Research; open HTML. Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, and Strengths and Limitations were read.
- **Question, sample, and outcome:** Time pressure → exhaustion → productivity loss. A total of 392 students at a German university completed three waves six months apart. Exhaustion was measured using the three-item MBI-9-SF exhaustion subscale, not the full burnout construct.
- **Method and split:** Cross-lagged panel SEM with alternative pathways; no independent predictive test set.
- **Reported results:** Time pressure at T1 was associated with exhaustion at T2, β = 0.13. Model M1 had CFI = 0.93 and RMSEA = 0.06. These are association and model-fit estimates, not ML performance measures.
- **Limitations:** Self-reports, selection of complete-panel participants, and sensitivity to the measurement interval. The design does not establish causality.
- **Project implication:** Supports examining time pressure and repeated measurements. A six-month finding does not establish a weekly prediction horizon.
- **Locators:** Study Procedure and Sample; Measures → Exhaustion; Results → Structural Equation Model; Strengths and Limitations.

## M-05 — Theory informing feature structure

**Reference:** Bakker, A. B., & Mostert, K. (2024). Study Demands–Resources Theory: Understanding Student Well-Being in Higher Education. *Educational Psychology Review, 36*, 92. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09940-8) · [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09940-8).

- **Publication and reading:** Peer-reviewed Review Article; open HTML. Student Well-Being, Study Demands and Resources, the relevant research-agenda discussion, and Traits Versus States in SD–R Theory were read.
- **Question and population:** A theoretical synthesis of higher-education research. It provides no new individual-level dataset, ML label, train/test split, or predictive metrics.
- **Method and contribution:** Develops Study Demands–Resources (SD–R) theory, distinguishing demands, resources, states, and behaviour, including feedback loops and short-term variation.
- **Limitations:** The propositions require further testing. The article is neither a deployable predictive model nor a systematic accuracy benchmark.
- **Project implication:** Provides a construct map and a reason to distinguish differences between students from changes within a student. It motivates, but does not validate, the proposed fields or observation interval.
- **Locators:** Study Demands and Resources → Proposition 1; Student Well-Being → Student Burnout; Traits Versus States in SD–R Theory.

## Critical synthesis and project decisions

1. **The outcome remains to be selected.** M-01–M-03 use different measurement frameworks, while M-04 measures exhaustion alone. The project should retain the instrument name, version, language, dimension, and scoring rules with each outcome. Scores from different instruments must not be pooled as an interchangeable label.
2. **Overload requires a separate operational definition.** SD–R distinguishes demands from resources (M-05). Study hours, deadlines, and perceived time pressure are separate candidate indicators in this project, not automatically a burnout scale.
3. **Longitudinal data are necessary for the proposed within-student forecasting study, but are not sufficient.** M-03 illustrates why repeated cross-sections must not be described as a linked panel. M-04 has a panel but a different horizon and no out-of-sample ML evaluation. Student linkage and timestamps must be retained, with separate evaluation of generalisation to new students and future periods.
4. **Psychometric fit does not replace predictive evaluation.** The CFA/SEM results in M-01 and M-02 do not justify promises about application performance. A predictive model requires independently measured outcomes, appropriate baselines, and a predefined test set.
5. **Local transfer is unconfirmed.** These studies do not validate a Russian- or Kazakh-language questionnaire for AITU. Reproduction, translation, recall-period changes, and scoring permissions must be checked before real questionnaire items are deployed. Arbitrary red/amber thresholds are not introduced.
