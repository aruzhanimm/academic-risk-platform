# Research Instruments

## Student questionnaire

The [published form](https://forms.gle/LZZL73VuXMRriJzs7) is in Russian. The English descriptions below document its content; they are not a separate administered translation or a psychological scale. The form introduces a student project about planning academic workload, voluntary participation and use of aggregate findings. It asks respondents not to include identifying or medical details.

The participation acknowledgement is required. The eight substantive questions are optional.

| Question | Topic and response format |
| --- | --- |
| Q1 | How tasks and deadlines are currently tracked. Multiple selection: learning system, calendar, task application, notes, memory, no separate tracking or another method. |
| Q2 | Frequency of difficulty understanding upcoming tasks and deadlines during the previous two study weeks. One response from never to almost always, with no-assignment and unsure options. |
| Q3 | What works or gets in the way in the current planning method, with an optional recent example. Free text. |
| Q4 | Information missing about upcoming workload, including the possibility that nothing is missing. Free text. |
| Q5 | Information the respondent would enter: tasks/deadlines, approximate study time, own workload rating, nothing or another answer. Multiple selection. |
| Q6 | Comfortable frequency for a short entry, including daily, several times weekly, weekly, when needed, no regular entry or unsure. One response. |
| Q7 | Who else could see records and under what conditions; nobody is an acceptable answer. Free text. |
| Q8 | Useful information about contacting university support, without describing personal health information or past consultations. Free text. |

Names, email addresses, group identifiers, grades and health histories are not requested. Direct quotations require separate permission. The form collects preferences about a possible workflow; it does not measure burnout or supply model labels.

## Discussion topics

The supplied interview records use a brief introduction asking permission to retain anonymised notes, with the option to skip questions or stop. The main topics are:

1. How assignments and deadlines were tracked during a recent study week.
2. One concrete planning difficulty, or what helped when there was no difficulty.
3. What is convenient or inconvenient in the current method.
4. Missing information, if any.
5. Information the participant would enter, preferred frequency and reasons for discontinuing use.
6. Who else might see records and under what conditions.

Follow-up questions sought concrete examples and checked the summary. Prototype activities concerned locating recent information, explaining an unavailable estimate and interpreting a deletion request. The [interview synthesis](interview_synthesis.md) distinguishes what the notes report from what the current prototype actually does, and records the limits of the session metadata.
