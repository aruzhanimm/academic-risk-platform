# 03. Critical Literature Review

Original source checks: **21 October 2026, Astana time**. English package prepared: **22 October 2026**.

This review substantively uses **13 unique sources: 10 peer-reviewed scientific publications**—nine journal articles and one conference paper—**and three official technical documents**. Technical documentation is counted separately from scientific publications, and a preprint and its published version are not counted twice. The search was targeted and thematic; it does not claim systematic or exhaustive coverage. Two foundational publications from 2017 and two methodologically relevant publications from 2021 fall outside the strict five-year window. Their specific roles are explained below; this is not a review limited to the latest publications.

The [research protocol](../research/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md) defines the selection approach, the [search log](../research/search_log.md) records the searches performed, and the [bibliography](../research/bibliography.md) provides metadata, reading locations, and access links. Detailed evidence cards cover [measurement, M-01–M-05](../research/contributions/measurement_review.md), and [machine learning, ML-01–ML-05](../research/contributions/ml_validation_review.md). All numerical findings below are reported by the cited authors; they are not project experiment results. No longitudinal burnout dataset has been collected and no project model has been trained. Initial user-needs evidence is reported separately in [Section 2](02_user_interviews.md).

## 1. Measurement: define the outcome before building the model

M-01–M-05 support connections among study demands, resources, and student well-being, but use different operational definitions. BAT-S, MBI-SS, the Study Burnout Inventory, and an exhaustion subscale are not automatically interchangeable labels. M-01 evaluates instrument structure in a specific Chilean sample. The lower reliability of mental distance relative to the overall score shows why an aggregate reliability statistic cannot represent every dimension (Results → Reliability of the scores). M-04 measures exhaustion; its findings cannot be extended to every component of burnout (Measures → Exhaustion).

M-02 and M-05 support considering demands and resources as separate factor groups rather than reducing overload to assignment counts. However, the high SEM R² values in M-02 describe associations in the study sample, not a future application's prediction error (Results → Main analysis: Model estimation). Similarly, the CFI and RMSEA values in M-01 do not replace ML generalisation testing. These studies do not establish the validity of a new combination of questions or an untested translation.

**Proposed project decision:** record the instrument, version, language, dimension, recall period, and scoring rules alongside each outcome; do not combine incompatible scales. Check use and translation permissions and population suitability before collection. Operationalise overload separately from burnout; do not relabel stress, dropout, or GPA as burnout. If an outcome is calculated from questionnaire items, those same items are not independent predictors of that outcome. The [feature operationalisation register](../research/feature_operationalization.md) contains proposals, not a validated questionnaire.

## 2. Time and data: current assessment does not establish forecasting

The closest domain-specific ML example, ML-01, uses a single cross-sectional survey. Its random 70/30 split evaluates generalisation within that sampling context, not prediction beyond the moment of data entry. AUC 0.852 belongs to a particular label, population, and split; it cannot be compared directly with psychometric fit or promised to users of this project (ML-01, Data analysis and Limitations).

The contrast between M-03 and M-04 is particularly relevant. The three dates in M-03 refer to separate cross-sectional samples; the authors explicitly identify this limitation (§12). M-04 links measurements from the same students over time, but estimates associations at six-month intervals without an independent ML test set. Neither multiple cross-sectional surveys nor panel SEM alone establish the suitability of a specific short-term forecast horizon for the proposed service.

OULAD (ML-02, Data Records, pp. 4–6) provides a useful temporal structure for academic events, but its different outcome makes it an unsuitable substitute for burnout data. A public archive and a large row count address availability, not alignment with the research question. If OULAD is used for a different benchmark, `final_result`, late `date_unregistration` values, and future events require explicit availability checks. This is a project inference from the documented schema, not a leakage experiment reported by the dataset authors.

**Proposed project decision:** use the student at time `t` as the unit of analysis, include only information available by `t`, and measure an agreed outcome at `t+h`. The horizon is unresolved: 28 days in the feature register is a candidate for discussion, not a duration established by the literature. Verify actual links between repeated observations, availability timestamps, and overlap between recall periods. The [dataset audit](../research/dataset_audit.md) currently assesses metadata, not individual records. Without a suitable panel, prepare a limited prototype and agree on data collection or a revised research formulation; cross-sectional data cannot establish future forecasting performance.

## 3. Validation and explainability are separate questions

The taxonomy in ML-03 distinguishes leakage through preprocessing or feature selection, inadmissible predictors, temporal dependence, and non-independent observations (Results → taxonomy, L1.2–L1.4, L2, L3.1–L3.3). All four are relevant here: observations may repeat within students, windows may overlap, answers may directly determine scoring, and fitted preprocessing statistics may incorporate test information. The paper demonstrates consequences for scientific comparisons, but its 294 affected studies do not estimate the percentage of errors across all research.

The unclear feature-selection description in ML-01 warrants a reproducibility check; it does not establish that leakage occurred. The paper reports different variable counts in Methods and Results, and the ordering of univariate selection relative to the split is not fully transparent (PDF pp. 4–5). This uncertainty should remain explicit rather than be resolved through assumptions about the authors' experiment.

Future evaluation should distinguish prediction for **new students** from prediction for **future periods**, then choose a split for each intended use. Fit all learned transformations inside the training data or training folds, and reserve the test set for final evaluation. Compare simple baselines and candidate models on the same observations with the same tuning budget. Where historical measurements exist, a last-observation baseline—predicting that the next value equals the latest known value—tests whether new factors add value beyond persistence of the measured state. These are proposed evaluation rules motivated by the literature, not completed experiments.

ML-04 explains model outputs through feature attributions. Its local accuracy property concerns consistency between the sum of attributions and the model output, not accuracy against an independent outcome (§3). ML-05 shows why dependent features require care when approximating Shapley values (§§3–4). Study hours, deadlines, time pressure, and previous scores may be related; the background distribution and the interpretation of absent features can change an explanation. Even stable attributions describe a model, not causes of burnout or the effectiveness of a recommendation.

**Proposed project decision:** specify the target, split, baselines, primary metric, missing-data handling, and uncertainty intervals before experimentation. MAE and RMSE are candidates for a continuous outcome; classification requires a separately justified threshold and calibration assessment. Evaluate predictive performance, technical explanation stability, and user understanding separately. The [research protocol](../research/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md) and [feature register](../research/feature_operationalization.md) document the relevant evidence requirements and unresolved choices. These evaluations have not yet been performed.

## Research gap and testable direction

The reviewed corpus includes psychometric studies, demands–resources theory, a panel study of exhaustion, a cross-sectional ML model, and methodological research. It **does not establish** that this project already has access to a longitudinal sample with an appropriate future burnout outcome and demonstrated transferability to AITU students. This is a bounded conclusion about the reviewed evidence, not a claim that no one has addressed a similar problem.

The proposed research question is: **Do previously measured demands and resources provide additional information about a future, agreed outcome beyond the academic calendar and the latest known state, under evaluation across time and students?** The answer may be negative. The intended contribution is a testable formulation, an audit of data suitability, a reproducible comparison, and a clear account of limitations. This review does not establish scientific novelty.

Before training, the project must resolve the instrument, permissions and language, data access and consent or other applicable permissions, an independent outcome, the forecast horizon, sufficient repeated observations, and the evaluation design. The evidence cards and synthesis still require substantive review, resolution of comments, and agreement with the supervisor on scope. A literature review does not replace interviews, user evaluation, or received feedback.
